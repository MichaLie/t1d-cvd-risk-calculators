Type 1 Diabetes — Cardiovascular Risk Meta-Calculator

Runs implemented published CV risk calculators side by side for an adult with type 1 diabetes, shows where they agree and where they diverge, and lets you recalibrate to local risk. It is not a new prediction model — it reproduces existing tools transparently (every coefficient is open in models.js; validation status is reported per endpoint). Decision support only; not a substitute for clinical judgement or a regulated medical device.

Methods, validation & coefficients (transparency)

Each tool below is a transparent re-implementation of a published equation. The Node harness includes source-paper worked examples, live-tool checks where available, and regression guards (36/36 checks pass; see test_models.js). Coefficients are fully open in models.js. The Steno-IHD/stroke endpoint is included for transparency but remains flagged as pending external/live-tool validation. This is an agreement/decision-support tool, not an accuracy claim: it shows how published tools differ on the same patient; it does not establish which is correct (that needs outcome data).

Recalibration. The SCORE2 family is recalibrated by risk region (a published mechanism). For the T1D-specific tools, the optional observed/expected ratio applies a transparent cumulative-hazard scaling — set it from your local registry's O/E (e.g. ~0.55 reproduces the Italian Steno over-estimation). Default 1.0 = published calibration.

Reproducibility note. The Scottish–Swedish tool here uses the published final-model coefficients (incl. the age-at-entry term omitted from the supplementary table) with a calibration constant (×1.32) fit to the authors' deployed app, because the published cubic/interaction coefficients are rounded to three decimals. Deprivation is fixed at quintile 3.

Open-source, self-contained (no data leaves the browser). Built as a companion to a review of CV risk calculators in T1D. All equations are human-traceable to their sources. Not for clinical use without local validation and regulatory approval.
