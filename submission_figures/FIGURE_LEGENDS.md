# Figure legends

Titles and legends are supplied separately from the image files. Artwork contains data labels, axes, visual keys and workflow content. Sources are identified by author and year; DOI links and figure-data locators are provided in `eval/figure_data.json` and `METHODS.md`.

## Figure 1. Development of selected cardiovascular and complication risk models

Models are positioned by publication year and grouped by their development population: type 1 diabetes (T1D), type 2 diabetes (T2D), or general/mixed populations. Symbol area is a monotonic logarithmic function of derivation cohort size, not proportional to participant count; vertical staggering only separates labels. This is a selected historical overview, not a systematic inventory or a ranking of clinical suitability. Years follow the named publication or guideline: PCE denotes the 2013 guideline, and PREVENT the 2024 journal issue (published online in 2023). UKPDS denotes the original CHD model (4,540 participants), not a combined CHD/stroke derivation sample. EURODIAB predicts a broader composite of major complications and death, not CVD alone. Model horizons include 5 years for Cederholm, 5/10 years for Steno, 3/5/7 years for EURODIAB, 4 years for ADVANCE, and 10 years for Pittsburgh, Scottish–Swedish, SCORE2, SCORE2-Diabetes, PCE, QRISK3 and Framingham. DIAL and LIFE-T1D also estimate lifetime risk; PREVENT provides 10/30-year estimates, and UKPDS uses a variable horizon. Cohort sizes and source locators are supplied in the accompanying figure data. Sources: Stevens et al. (2001), D’Agostino et al. (2008), Zgibor et al. (2010), Cederholm et al. (2011), Kengne et al. (2011), Goff et al. (2013), Soedamah-Muthu et al. (2014), Vistisen et al. (2016), Hippisley-Cox et al. (2017), Berkelmans et al. (2019), SCORE2 Working Group (2021), McGurnaghan et al. (2021), SCORE2-Diabetes Working Group (2023), Helmink et al. (2024) and Khan et al. (2024).

## Figure 2. Predictor domains in selected cardiovascular and complication risk models

A filled cell indicates that at least one predictor from the domain enters the named model; an empty cell indicates that the domain is absent from that specification. Row-label colours distinguish development populations. Domains combine related inputs and do not imply identical coefficients, definitions or importance. “Current age / sex” includes either an explicit term, sex-specific modelling or an age time scale. Cederholm includes age at onset and duration, but no explicit current-age or sex term. “Other” includes body size, exercise, ethnicity/race, deprivation, atrial fibrillation, prior CVD, insulin treatment or other comorbidities, depending on the model. BP/lipid treatment does not include insulin treatment or modelled treatment benefits. Diabetes status/type is shown separately: QRISK3 distinguishes T1D from T2D, whereas PCE, PREVENT and Framingham use diabetes status; a fixed diabetes population is not an additional predictor. UKPDS combines the CHD and stroke predictor sets. PREVENT denotes the base total-CVD specification, excluding optional HbA1c, albuminuria and deprivation extensions and the HF-specific BMI term; Framingham denotes the laboratory-based model. EURODIAB predicts broader major outcomes rather than CVD alone. These specifications do not include CGM time in range, hypoglycaemia exposure, autonomic neuropathy or insulin-delivery modality. Sources are the model publications identified in Figure 1 and Kothari et al. (2002) for UKPDS stroke. Abbreviations: BP, blood pressure; CVD, cardiovascular disease; CGM, continuous glucose monitoring; HF, heart failure; BMI, body mass index; HbA1c, glycated haemoglobin.

## Figure 3. Reported discrimination of selected risk models in type 1 diabetes

Each point is a reported C-statistic from the labelled model and cohort. Squares indicate development or internal validation, circles indicate validation in a separate dataset, and diamonds indicate published pooled estimates. No confidence intervals or synthetic midpoint estimates are plotted. Horizontal separators distinguish cardiovascular endpoints, the broader EURODIAB outcome, and the meta-analysis summaries. *EURODIAB predicts major coronary heart disease, stroke, end-stage renal failure, amputations, blindness and all-cause death; its values must not be interpreted as discrimination for CVD alone. Cederholm includes participants with and without previous CVD and uses different derivation and validation horizons. Pittsburgh entries are sex-specific external validation results, not derivation estimates. The Scottish estimate for the Scottish–Swedish model represents development-population performance; the original model is represented in the literature appraisal but excluded from the repository’s numerical comparison. Erqou’s pooled estimates reproduce that review’s model grouping and are not a new meta-analysis. Differences in populations, endpoints, follow-up and validation design preclude a direct ranking. Sources: Cederholm et al. (2011), Vistisen et al. (2016), Zgibor et al. (2010), McGurnaghan et al. (2021), Helmink et al. (2024), Soedamah-Muthu et al. (2014) and Erqou et al. (2025). EDC, Epidemiology of Diabetes Complications; CACTI, Coronary Artery Calcification in Type 1 Diabetes; FinnDiane, Finnish Diabetic Nephropathy Study.

## Figure 4. Agreement between implemented models across common analytic risk bands

Linearly weighted Cohen’s κ compares predicted 10-year risks assigned to common bands of <10%, 10 to <20%, and ≥20% in 10,000 assumption-based synthetic T1D profiles (seed 20260613). Each pair uses only profiles with finite outputs from both models, so sample sizes vary across cells. The arithmetic mean across the 45 distinct model pairs is 0.43 (range 0.02–0.78); diagonal entries compare a model with itself. These analytic bands are not universal treatment thresholds. The models predict different endpoints, and the cohort contains no observed clinical outcomes: agreement therefore measures neither calibration nor clinical accuracy. SCORE2-family calculations use the high-risk region setting. *ADVANCE uses a reconstructed centring constant and extrapolates its native 4-year output to 10 years under a constant-hazard assumption. †UKPDS-CVD combines paper-based CHD and stroke risks under an independence approximation; an alternative Oxford stroke-parameter specification is reported as a sensitivity analysis. The sensitivity analyses examine these implementation choices. Steno IHD/stroke denotes its narrower endpoint. Source: the reproducible evaluation outputs; pair-specific sample sizes and full-precision κ are generated by the figure build command. IHD, ischaemic heart disease. QRISK3 results are subject to the [ClinRisk disclaimer](../metatool/qrisk3-notice.html).

## Figure 5. Distributions of predicted risk in the synthetic type 1 diabetes cohort

Boxes show the median and interquartile range of predicted 10-year risk; whiskers extend to the most extreme observed values within 1.5 interquartile ranges of the box. Outlier points are not drawn. The full 0–100% probability axis is displayed. Rows are ordered by median risk and use each model’s eligible subset of the same 10,000 synthetic profiles (seed 20260613); sample sizes are given in the row labels. The dashed and dash-dot lines mark the 10% and 20% analytic boundaries, respectively, rather than clinical treatment thresholds. Colours distinguish model development populations. Different eligible populations and endpoints make the percentages non-interchangeable; the graph does not establish overestimation or underestimation against clinical outcomes. SCORE2-family calculations use the high-risk region setting. *ADVANCE is extrapolated from 4 to 10 years using constant hazard and a reconstructed centring constant. †UKPDS-CVD approximates combined CHD/stroke risk under independence, using the paper-based equations; an Oxford stroke-parameter alternative is reported separately. These qualifications apply to the implementations plotted here, not to the validity of the original authors’ models. Source: reproducible evaluation outputs; medians, quartiles, whiskers and eligible sample sizes are generated by the figure build command. IHD, ischaemic heart disease. QRISK3 results are subject to the [ClinRisk disclaimer](../metatool/qrisk3-notice.html).

## Figure 6. A guideline-aware approach to cardiovascular risk discussion in type 1 diabetes

This four-step workflow is an author synthesis for clinical discussion, not a validated decision rule or an endorsement of the repository calculator for clinical use. Prevention needs, established atherosclerotic cardiovascular disease (ASCVD) and organ damage should be assessed before deciding whether a numerical estimate adds value. Guidance differs: the 2023 ESC diabetes guideline states that the Scottish–Swedish model may be considered for 10-year risk estimation in T1D (class IIb, level B), whereas NICE NG238 recommendation 1.1.9 advises against using a risk assessment tool in people with T1D. If scoring is appropriate under the applicable guideline, select a model with relevant endpoints, prediction horizon, eligibility and population validation. Steno and LIFE-T1D provide additional published T1D-specific approaches, including lifetime-risk framing for LIFE-T1D; their existence does not imply universal guideline endorsement. Risk estimates should inform shared discussion without overriding treatment indications. Sources: Marx et al. (2023), section 11 and Recommendation Table 26; NICE NG238, recommendation 1.1.9; Vistisen et al. (2016); Helmink et al. (2024). The Scottish–Swedish model cited by ESC is not implemented in this repository.
